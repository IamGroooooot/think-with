# Research discovery

Identify the situation, desired change, and remaining unknowns from existing
context. Start searching from the user's question, assuming relevant research
may exist. If initial results are thin, broaden through research terms or the
references of relevant papers.

Choose fields by problem relevance and accumulated research, rather than word
matches or familiar labels. Open original sources to check what was studied,
how evidence has accumulated, and how the user's situation differs. Explanatory
research can help; a review label alone does not establish quality.

Revise candidates when new evidence warrants it. When further searching no
longer changes the selection, explain the search limits. Failed retrieval does
not establish that a research field or relevant literature is absent.

## Before expert briefing

If an unresolved fact could change the advice or next inquiry, briefly identify
one such fact, two plausible answers, and what would change under each. Route it
to public evidence, a user question or local observation, or conditional advice
when it cannot presently be resolved. Include it in the existing expert brief;
the expert may revise or reject this framing.

Skip this operation when no consequential gap remains or discovery already
covered it. It requires no extra agent or automatic search. Preserve useful
research outside this question, settled causes, and open learning requests;
continue actual consultation and final checking.
